# Agentic AI Engineering Curriculum

## Curriculum structure

The project currently follows 13 disciplines. The sequence is a learning structure; connections between disciplines will be revisited where necessary.

## Discipline descriptions

### 1. Agent Architecture and Design
Understand what an agent is, its components, identity, boundaries, lifecycle, roles, responsibilities and autonomy.

### 2. Reasoning and Planning
Study how agents interpret objectives, reason through problems, decompose tasks, plan, select actions and revise plans.

### 3. Context, Memory and Knowledge
Study how agents use immediate context, persistent memory, external knowledge, retrieval and information management.

### 4. Tools and Environment Engineering
Understand how agents interact with tools, APIs, software, external services and environments, including capability and permission boundaries.

### 5. Execution and Loop Engineering
Study the operational cycle through which an agent observes, decides, acts, receives feedback, checks outcomes and continues or stops.

### 6. Orchestration and Multi-Agent Systems
Study task allocation, coordination, communication, delegation, dependencies and collaboration among multiple agents.

### 7. Security, Safety and Governance
Study threats, permissions, safeguards, policy enforcement, risk controls and safe operation.

### 8. Agentic Governance and Self-Governing Systems
Study how autonomous and multi-agent systems can operate under shared rules, distributed authority, accountability, collective decision-making and conflict-resolution mechanisms. This discipline also informs the other disciplines.

### 9. Evaluation and Testing
Study how to test agent behaviour, assess task success, measure quality and reliability, identify failure modes and validate changes.

### 10. Human-Agent Interaction
Study how people instruct, supervise, collaborate with, approve actions from and intervene in agentic systems.

### 11. Observability and Reliability
Study how to understand system behaviour, trace actions, monitor performance, detect failures and support dependable operation.

### 12. Performance and Infrastructure
Study the resources, runtime environments, scaling, latency, cost and infrastructure needed to operate agentic systems.

### 13. Evolution and Continuous Improvement
Study how agentic systems are maintained, updated, evaluated and improved without losing control, continuity or accumulated knowledge.

## Cross-cutting topics

Some topics span several disciplines rather than belonging exclusively to one chapter. Agentic Governance is explicitly cross-cutting. Other cross-cutting topics may be added when the learning establishes their relevance.

## Curriculum change control

The curriculum can evolve when new evidence or a genuine conceptual gap warrants it. A proposed addition should explain:
- What capability or knowledge gap it addresses.
- Whether it belongs in an existing discipline or needs a distinct discipline.
- Which other disciplines it affects.
- Why adding it is preferable to simply expanding an existing chapter.

Record substantive changes in [Decision Log](DECISIONS.md) and [Changelog](CHANGELOG.md).
