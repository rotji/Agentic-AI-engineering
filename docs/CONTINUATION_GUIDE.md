# Continuation Guide

## 1. Resume the learning sequence

Before continuing a session:

1. Read [Current State](CURRENT_STATE.md).
2. Open the relevant chapter document from the current discipline guide under `docs/disciplines/`.
3. Continue from the next unfinished subsection.
4. Do not repeat completed lessons unless the user asks to revisit or deepen them.

Current continuation point: **Discipline 2 → Chapter 2 → 2.2 Interpreting Instructions and Identifying Intent.** Chapter 1 is complete; subsection 2.1 is covered.

## 2. Document a lesson

After a chapter or subsection is covered:

- Add or update its chapter document.
- Preserve definitions, distinctions, examples, principles and important limitations.
- Mark illustrative frameworks as illustrative; do not present them as universal standards.
- Keep unresolved questions separate from established content.
- Update [Current State](CURRENT_STATE.md) to show the exact next step.
- Add a concise entry to [Changelog](CHANGELOG.md) when the project state or documentation materially changes.
- Update [README](../README.md) or [Start Here](../START_HERE.md) only when navigation or top-level status changes.

## 3. Integrate learning with practical building

The project is not a theory-only course. The 13 disciplines organise knowledge, but practical work may progress alongside the curriculum.

Use the [Practical Build and Sector Transfer Method](PRACTICAL_BUILD_AND_SECTOR_TRANSFER.md):

- Begin with a real problem in the agricultural pilot path.
- Study the concepts needed to understand and address it.
- Define requirements, capabilities and acceptance conditions.
- Discover and assess existing solutions before considering custom development.
- Assemble suitable existing solutions and test their fit.
- Build only where a genuine gap or clearly justified reason remains.
- Record practical progress separately from lesson completion.
- Assess whether demonstrated principles can transfer to other sectors, adapting and validating them independently.

Do not let a passing mention of a technology turn into an unrelated survey. Investigate tools when a concrete capability requirement makes them relevant, not simply because they are advertised or recommended. Keep proposals, demonstrations and tested working capabilities clearly distinguished.

## 4. Handle new concepts

When a new concept appears:

1. Identify the capability or problem it addresses.
2. Determine whether it belongs in the current chapter, another discipline, or a cross-cutting topic.
3. Explain the relationship to concepts already documented.
4. Record a curriculum or scope change only when there is a substantive reason.
5. Preserve the previous learning record and explain any change rather than silently rewriting history.

## 5. Maintain repository continuity

The repository should allow a reader to understand:
- What the project is about.
- What curriculum is being followed.
- What has already been covered.
- Which decisions and constraints are active.
- Exactly where to continue.

The repository is the durable project record. A conversation is a place to work through the material, not the only place where its knowledge should exist.
